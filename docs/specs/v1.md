# Rootly v1 — Spec

Status: ready-for-agent
Source: `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md`, `GLOSSARY.md`, and ADR-0001 through ADR-0010.

## Problem Statement

People who keep houseplants have no single, low-effort place to remember when each Plant needs watering or fertilizing, record what they did, and see how their Plants change over time. The tools that do help with care are private notebooks: they give nothing back. The tools that show other people's plants are discovery-first: they treat care as an afterthought, and they often make the owner's precise home location public to do it.

An Owner with a dozen Plants cannot tell at a glance which Plants are due today, cannot trust a reminder that names "the Monstera" when three Plants are named Monstera, and has no safe way to show off a thriving Plant to people nearby without revealing where they live. A Visitor who wants to see what grows in their neighborhood has no map that shows real, individual Plants at a safe distance from anyone's home.

## Solution

Rootly is a cross-platform mobile app with two equally primary, deliberately separate loops:

- **Care**: every Owner has one flat list of Plants. Each Plant has a personal name, an optional Species, an optional photo, and a Care Schedule for watering and fertilizing. The Owner logs Care Actions, which reset the next due date, and receives one daily digest at a time they choose. A Catalog Species supplies curated Care Card guidance; a Custom Species supplies none, so the Owner sets the intervals. Growth Entries record dated photos, Measurements, notes, and Events, and numeric Measurements can be charted over time.
- **Discovery**: a Plant is private by default. The Owner may make it public one Plant at a time. A Public Plant appears on the Owner's Profile and as a pin on the Map, but only at the Owner's Coarse Area — one roughly 1 km grid cell shared by all of that Owner's Public Plants, so the Map cannot be used to triangulate a home. Exact coordinates are never stored. Discovery is read-only: Map, Public Profiles, and a global recent feed. Visitors can browse without an account and can filter by Catalog Species.

The care loop is validated first. Discovery is shipped read-only and may be sparse at launch. The product is free in v1, English-only, and available globally.

## User Stories

### Identity and access

1. As a Visitor, I want to browse the Map and public Profiles without an account, so that I can see what the product offers before signing up.
2. As a Visitor, I want to open a shareable Profile URL containing a Username, so that I can reach an Owner's Profile directly.
3. As an Owner, I want to sign in with Google, Apple, or an email one-time code, so that I can use the sign-in method I already trust.
4. As an Owner, I want a unique Username displayed with `@`, so that people can find and share my Profile.
5. As an Owner, I want a free-form Display Name that does not have to be my real name, so that I can control how I am identified.
6. As an Owner, I want an optional avatar, so that my Profile feels personal.
7. As an Owner, I want to change my Display Name at any time, so that it can stay current.
8. As an Owner, I want to change my Username, so that I am not stuck with an early choice.
9. As an Owner, I want my old Username reserved for 30 days after a change, so that someone else cannot immediately impersonate me.
10. As a Visitor, I want a changed Username not to redirect to the new one, so that an old link does not silently resolve to a different identity.
11. As an Owner, I want to recover access through the same sign-in methods, so that losing one device does not lock me out.
12. As an Owner who has lost all access, I want support to verify me and restore my account manually, so that recovery is possible without security questions.
13. As a new Owner, I want to self-attest that I am 16 or older, so that sign-up stays simple while eligibility is explicit.
14. As an Owner, I want to re-authenticate before account deletion, so that someone holding my unlocked phone cannot delete my history.

### Plants

15. As an Owner, I want one flat list of my Plants, so that I do not have to organize them into Collections, Balconies, or Gardens.
16. As an Owner, I want to add a Plant with a mandatory personal name, so that every Plant is recognizable to me.
17. As an Owner, I want Plant names up to 60 characters, so that descriptive names fit.
18. As an Owner, I want Plant names not to be unique, so that I can name two Plants the same if that is how I think about them.
19. As an Owner, I want to rename a Plant at any time, so that its name can follow reality.
20. As an Owner, I want to add an optional photo to a Plant, so that I can recognize it visually.
21. As an Owner, I want to add a Plant without a Species, so that an unknown plant is still usable.
22. As an Owner, I want to edit a Plant's details later, so that early guesses can be corrected.
23. As an Owner, I want to archive a Plant, so that a retired or dead Plant leaves my active list without losing its history.
24. As an Owner, I want archiving to stop reminders, remove the Plant from my active list, and unpublish it, so that an archived Plant creates no ongoing work or exposure.
25. As an Owner, I want to restore an archived Plant, so that archiving is reversible.
26. As an Owner, I want to permanently delete a Plant after confirmation, so that I can remove it completely.
27. As an Owner, I want permanent deletion to remove the Plant's care logs and Growth Entries, so that nothing is left behind.

30. As an Owner, I want a Custom Species free-text fallback, so that plants outside the catalog are still supported.

34. As an Owner, I want to change a Plant's Species later, so that a correction updates its guidance.
35. As a Visitor, I want Public Plants to show their Species, or "Species unknown" when absent, so that discovery has useful context.

## Implementation Decisions

### Platform and runtime

- Client: a React Native app built with Expo, targeting iOS and Android. Map rendering uses `@maplibre/maplibre-react-native`, which requires an Expo development build rather than Expo Go. iOS and Android are shipped together.
- Backend: Supabase provides Postgres, Auth, Storage, Row-Level Security, and PostGIS. The client talks to Postgres and Storage through Supabase's generated API; privacy is enforced by RLS, not by client-side filtering.
- Maps: MapLibre renders a self-hosted PMTiles archive of OpenStreetMap-derived data served from object storage/CDN. Protomaps planet PMTiles on Cloudflare R2 with OpenStreetMap/Protomaps attribution. No map vendor API, no place search, no geocoding.
- Push: Expo Push for the daily digest. An Expo push token is stored per device per Owner.

### Domain modules

The implementation is organized around these modules, each with a pure or near-pure core so that behavior can be tested without a device or a database:

- **Identity**: sign-in methods, Username uniqueness and reservation, Display Name, avatar, eligibility attestation, timezone, account deletion and recovery handoff.
- **Plants**: the flat Plant list, personal names, optional Species and photo, visibility, archive/restore/delete, and lifecycle rules.
- **Species**: Catalog Species records with Care Cards, search over common and scientific names, Custom Species fallback, and "Other" grouping.
- **Care**: Care Schedules per Plant per Care Action, interval resolution (Species default, per-Plant override, Custom Species default), due-date calculation, Care Logs, undo, edit, delete.
- **Reminders**: the in-app due list and the daily digest composition (due + overdue grouping, Plant names, local send time, timezone re-localization).
- **Growth**: Growth Entries, Measurements with units, notes, Events, and chart series derivation from numeric Measurements.
- **Visibility and location**: Plant visibility, the one Coarse Area per Owner, grid-cell derivation from a map tap, and the constant shared-area invariant.
- **Discovery**: radius queries over Public Plants, Species filters, the global recent feed with 30-day window and all-time fallback, and first-publish ordering.
- **Safety**: blocks and reports, content exclusion for the blocking Visitor, and the manual moderation queue.
- **Media**: client-side compression, upload size limit, per-Owner storage cap enforcement, and placeholder selection.
- **Notifications**: permission timing, digest scheduling, push delivery, and failure handling.
- **Analytics**: the four first-party events, with no PII and no precise location.

### Data model and invariants

- An Owner profile is keyed to the auth user and carries a unique Username, Display Name, optional avatar, timezone, notification time, self-attested eligibility, and one Coarse Area.
- A Username change writes a reservation row for the old Username with a 30-day expiry. Resolution of a Username never redirects a reserved name to the new one.
- A Plant belongs to exactly one Owner. It has a personal name (1–60 characters, not unique), an optional Species reference or Custom Species text, an optional primary photo, a visibility flag, and an archived flag. Archiving also clears visibility in the same operation.
- A Plant references either a Catalog Species or a Custom Species name, never both.
- A Care Schedule is one row per Plant per Care Action (watering, fertilizing) with an interval in days and a next-due date. Custom Species defaults are 7 days for watering and 30 days for fertilizing at creation; Catalog Species defaults come from the catalog. The Owner can override either interval.
- Logging a Care Action inserts a Care Log at the server's current time and recomputes the next-due date from the interval. Undo reverses both within 5 seconds. Timestamps are immutable and cannot be backdated; edit and delete are allowed on the log.
- An archived Plant is excluded from the active list, due lists, digest composition, and all discovery surfaces. Restore reverses this except that visibility must be re-enabled deliberately.
- A Growth Entry belongs to one Plant, has a date, and must carry at least one of photo, Measurement, note, or Event. Measurements are independently optional height/length, width, and leaf count, with a length unit of cm or inches. Events are a fixed enum: repotted, pruned, flowered, propagated, pest/disease, other.
- The Coarse Area is stored as a grid-cell identifier and/or cell geometry on the Owner, not as a free coordinate. The grid is roughly 1 km. All Public Plants read the Owner's current Coarse Area at query time, so changing it moves every Public Plant atomically.
- Exact device or Plant coordinates are never written to the database. A map tap is converted to a cell at the edge before any write. The client must never send raw coordinates in a write payload.
- A Public Plant appears on the Map and on the Profile through the same visibility flag. There is no profile-only state.
- A Profile is discoverable only when the Owner has at least one Public Plant.
- Feed ordering uses a first-published timestamp that is set once and never updated by edits, unpublish, or republish. The recent feed uses a 30-day window and falls back to all-time when results are sparse.
- Blocks are Owner-to-Owner rows. Queries for a signed-in Visitor exclude Plants and Profiles of Owners they blocked. The blocked Owner cannot see the block.
- Reports reference a Public Plant or a Profile and enter a manually reviewed queue. Reported content may be removed, which unpublishes or hides it.
- Push tokens belong to a device and an Owner, and are removed on sign-out or account deletion.
- Account deletion cascades through Plants, care logs, Growth Entries, photos, push tokens, and public content; reserved Usernames are anonymized or released per the reservation policy. Backups age out within 30 days.
- Storage objects for photos are scoped per Owner, and the per-Owner 1 GB cap is enforced before issuing an upload.

### Privacy enforcement

- RLS policies are the single source of truth for who can read a Plant, care log, Growth Entry, photo, Profile, block, and report.
- Public read access is limited to Public Plants of non-archived Plants, their photo, personal name, Species display name, and the Owner's Display Name, avatar, and Coarse Area preview. Care data, Growth Entries, notes, Measurements, schedules, and exact coordinates are never exposed by a public policy.
- Discovery queries accept a Visitor location only at query time to select a radius; the location is not persisted. Radius options are 5, 10, 25, and 50 km with a 10 km default.
- The Map shows pins at the Coarse Area cell, not at Plant level. Multiple Public Plants in one cell render as distinct pins within that cell in a way that does not imply a more precise Plant location than the cell.
- Without location permission the Map opens at a default view and manual pan/zoom works; radius-based nearby queries are not offered.

### Interfaces between modules

- The Care module exposes interval resolution and next-due computation as pure functions that take a schedule and a timestamp and return a due date, so both the app and the digest job use one definition of "due".
- The Reminders module composes a digest from a list of due items (Plant name, Care Action, days overdue) and is independent of push transport.
- The Visibility module exposes grid-cell derivation from a map point and exposes the Owner's current Coarse Area to both Profile and Map read paths.
- The Growth module exposes a chart-series derivation from Measurements that filters by unit and ignores missing values.
- The Discovery module exposes feed ordering and filter grouping ("Other" for Custom Species) as pure functions over candidate Public Plants.
- The Media module exposes compression and cap checks before upload, with the cap check reading the Owner's current usage.
- Notifications are requested only after the first Plant and Care Schedule are saved; the permission prompt follows an explanatory screen.
- Analytics emits events at the four named moments with a non-identifying distinct ID and no location payload.

### Technical clarifications

- There is no offline write queue in v1. Reads use the last-synced cache; writes fail visibly when offline.
- There is no place search, geocoding, or neighborhood name anywhere in v1.
- The Map may be sparse at launch. Empty nearby results must offer the global feed and a call to publish a Plant.
- The app is English-only, worldwide, and free. No paywall, ads, or paid tiers.
- Username changes are allowed; Display Name changes are unrestricted.
- The Catalog is roughly 100 Species, curated and reviewed with source notes, and is the only source of Care Cards.
- Notification digest time follows the stored timezone and is re-localized on timezone change.

## Testing Decisions

### What makes a good test

Tests assert externally observable behavior: what an Owner or Visitor can see and do, and what the database returns to a given caller. Tests do not assert internal function calls, component structure, state shape, or query implementation. A test should fail when a user-visible rule breaks, and keep passing when the implementation is refactored. Privacy rules get the strictest treatment, because a passing test must prove absence as well as presence.

### Proposed seams

The repo is greenfield, so these are proposals. They aim for the fewest seams and the highest possible point:

1. **Application core seam (primary).** All care scheduling, due/overdue calculation, digest composition, feed ordering, filter grouping, grid-cell derivation, and chart series live in pure TypeScript modules behind small interfaces. Most behavior tests run here, with no device, network, or database.
2. **Database policy seam (privacy).** A local Supabase instance exercises RLS and PostGIS against real migrations. This is the highest seam that can prove privacy invariants, so it is worth having even though it is a second seam. Tests are written from the caller's role: anonymous Visitor, signed-in Owner, blocked Visitor, reported content.
3. **Screen-level acceptance seam (thin).** A small number of React Native Testing Library tests cover the highest-value journeys end to end at the screen boundary: activate (add Plant, accept schedule), log care with undo, publish a Plant with a Coarse Area, archive/restore. These assert rendered behavior only.
4. **Digest job seam.** The daily digest is a pure composition step wrapped by a thin scheduled job and push adapter. The composition is tested at seam 1; delivery failure handling is tested with a fake push transport.

Seams 1 and 2 should carry nearly all tests. Seam 3 exists to catch wiring mistakes, not to re-test domain rules. No snapshot tests of visual layout.

### Modules to be tested

- Care interval resolution and next-due computation for Catalog defaults, per-Plant overrides, and Custom Species defaults.
- Digest composition: due and overdue grouping, Plant names, overdue-by-N labeling, clearing after log or interval change, timezone re-localization.
- Visibility: grid-cell derivation, shared Coarse Area invariant, no exact-coordinate persistence, unpublish on archive, publish showing on both Map and Profile.
- Discovery: radius filtering, Species filter including "Other", recent feed ordering and 30-day window, all-time fallback, first-publish stability across edits/unpublish/republish, blocked and reported exclusion.
- RLS: anonymous read of public data only; Owner-only access to private Plants and all care and Growth data; block isolation; report queue access; deletion cascade.
- Growth: entry validity rule, Measurement unit handling, chart series over time, private-by-default.
- Account: Username uniqueness and 30-day reservation, no redirect, deletion cascade, re-authentication gate.
- Media: client compression, 10 MB upload limit, 1 GB cap blocking and recovery after deletion.

### Prior art

There is no prior art in this repository: v1 is the first implementation. The conventions chosen here — pure core modules, RLS integration tests against local Supabase, and a thin screen-acceptance layer — become the prior art for later features. ADR-0008 and ADR-0009 constrain the reminder and backend tests; ADR-0004 and ADR-0006 constrain the privacy tests.

## Out of Scope

- Automatic photo identification.
- Follows, reactions, comments, and direct messages.
- Collections, Balconies, or Gardens as entities.
- Public care logs, schedules, Measurements, notes, or Growth Entries.
- Exact Plant or Owner coordinates, place search, geocoding, or neighborhood names.
- A web app, non-English UI, or geographic gating.
- A paywall, ads, or paid tiers.
- Automated moderation, an appeals process, or automated report triage.
- An offline write queue.
- Per-action or immediate notifications; the daily digest is the only push.
- Care-log notes, long-form species articles, and external care links.
- In-app data export; export is manual through support.
- Security questions for recovery.
- Admin moderation tooling beyond the manual queue (still an open implementation item).
